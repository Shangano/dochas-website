Our services

Care that fits around the person

Care shouldn't begin with a standard package, it should begin with a conversation. Services depend on each person's assessed needs and what Dòchas is registered and able to provide.

Washing, showering, bathing, dressing, grooming, oral care and toileting, delivered sensitively. Every part of personal care protects privacy and dignity at every stage, and encourages independence wherever it's safe to do so. Staff explain what they're doing before they do it, and give people time rather than rushing.

Help with the ordinary things that make the biggest difference to remaining at home. This is never about taking control, it's about keeping everyday life manageable: light housework, laundry, and keeping on top of the small tasks that can otherwise pile up.

Full details →

We prepare food to your preferences, keep an eye on appetite, and notice, then record and report, changes like reduced appetite or difficulty eating and drinking, so they can be picked up early.

Full details →

Our trained care staff provide medication support including verbal prompting and prompt-and-assist support. Dòchas staff do not administer medication. Where medication administration is required, we work with the District Nursing Team or other appropriate healthcare professionals.

Full details →

Following assessed moving & handling guidance, we help people stay as independent as possible while reducing the risk of falls or injury, whether that's getting in and out of bed, moving between rooms, or getting outside.

Warm and interested, while remaining professional, this time belongs to the client. Companionship visits can include chatting, hobbies, games, or simply having someone there.

Full details →

Home care doesn't have to mean staying inside. We can support trips out, whether that's a GP appointment, a social club, or simply a walk, so people stay active, involved and connected to their community.

We work with the person, their family, and the professionals involved, so support is in place from the moment someone returns home, reducing the risk of readmission and easing the adjustment back to daily life.

Caring for a loved one is demanding, and everyone needs a break sometimes. Where appropriate, respite support gives family carers time away from caring responsibilities, without worry.

Who we support

People at every stage of needing a little more support

Older adults, adults with disabilities, people recovering from illness or surgery, people living with dementia, families and unpaid carers, and anyone finding daily life harder to manage.
