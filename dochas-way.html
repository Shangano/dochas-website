Our culture

The Dòchas Way

The Dòchas Way is the name we give to the principles that guide how we work. It is not one policy, it's our culture, how all our policies, standards, values and professional responsibilities come together in everyday care.

Our eight principles

Every visit belongs to the client

When a member of Dòchas staff enters someone's home, that visit is about the person receiving care: their needs, their wellbeing, their choices, their dignity, their comfort, their independence.

1

Every Visit Belongs to the Client

Our staff are expected to be friendly and warm, but professional. Clients should never become emotional support for care workers, be drawn into staff members' personal lives, or feel that professional boundaries have become unclear.

We want carers to listen more than they speak. To take an interest without becoming intrusive. To build trust without becoming overfamiliar. And to remember why they are there.

2

Person-Centred Care

No two people are exactly the same, so care should never be delivered as though they are. We take time to understand how someone prefers their day to begin, what they enjoy eating, what they can do independently, what they'd like help with, how they prefer to communicate, what makes them uncomfortable, what matters to them, who is important in their life, what risks need to be managed, and what outcomes they'd like their support to help them achieve.

Care plans provide direction, but the person remains at the centre.

3

Dignity

Dignity is one of the most important principles in everything we do. It means speaking to people respectfully, explaining what we're doing before we do it, protecting privacy during personal care, giving people time, avoiding unnecessary embarrassment, and respecting someone's home, belongings, culture, beliefs and identity.

And never forgetting that needing help does not make someone less entitled to choice, respect or control.

4

Independence

Our role is not to do everything for someone simply because we can. Where it's safe and appropriate, we encourage people to continue doing what they can for themselves, whether that's a little more time to dress independently, or preparing part of their own meal. Walking alongside rather than taking over. Offering support only where it's needed.

Independence looks different for every person. The important thing is that care should support independence wherever possible, rather than unnecessarily reducing it.

5

Choice and Control

People receiving care should remain involved in decisions about their own lives, both everyday choices and larger decisions. We listen. We explain. We provide information. We respect informed decisions.

There may sometimes be situations where a person's choice involves some level of risk. Where the individual has the capacity to make that decision, our role is not automatically to take the choice away. Instead, we help them understand the possible consequences, look at ways of reducing unnecessary risk, and record the decision appropriately. Safe care and personal choice should work together.

6

Compassion

Compassion is more than being nice. It means paying attention. Noticing when something has changed. Recognising when someone needs reassurance. Understanding that a task which feels routine to a care worker may feel deeply personal to the person receiving that care. It also means recognising when someone simply needs to be heard.

We want our staff to provide care with patience and genuine concern, while still maintaining clear professional boundaries.

7

Safety

Good intentions are not enough. Care must also be safe. We expect staff to follow care plans, risk assessments, moving and handling guidance, infection prevention procedures and other safety requirements. Staff are encouraged to stop and ask if something doesn't feel safe, and are expected to report changes, hazards, incidents and near misses. We would rather someone ask a question than guess.

Safety applies to clients and staff alike. A safe service protects everyone.

8

Professionalism

Care takes place inside people's private homes. That creates a level of access and trust that must never be taken lightly. Our staff may see personal belongings, family relationships, health information, medication, correspondence, finances and private moments.

That's why Dòchas places strong emphasis on professional boundaries, confidentiality, honesty, integrity, reliable attendance, accurate records, appropriate communication, respectful behaviour, safe handling of client property and money, and speaking up when something is wrong.

"Warm care and professional care are not opposites. At Dòchas, they belong together."

Who we support

Adults who need care or assistance in their own homes

Our service has a particular focus on older adults and people whose support needs affect their ability to manage some aspects of everyday life independently. The level of support required varies from person to person, some people need a small amount of help each day, others need more regular or structured support.

Before care begins, we take time to understand what support is required and whether Dòchas is the right service to provide it. We don't believe in promising services that fall outside our registration, competence or ability to provide safely.

Care in your own home

Home comes first, always

Home means different things to different people: a favourite chair, photographs, neighbours, a garden, routines, memories, independence. Whenever possible and appropriate, home care allows people to receive support without leaving all of that behind.

When Dòchas enters someone's home, we remember that it remains their home first and our workplace second. We respect the space, household routines and personal belongings, and remember that being invited into someone's home does not give us permission to become involved in every part of their private life.

Working with families

An important part of the support network

We value constructive relationships with relatives and representatives, and understand that families often know the person extremely well. At the same time, the individual receiving care remains at the centre of the service. We balance family involvement with the client's rights, wishes, consent, privacy and confidentiality.

Where appropriate, and with the necessary authority or consent, we work with families to share important information, discuss changes and support good outcomes.

Working with professionals

Home care rarely exists in isolation

Depending on the person's needs, Dòchas may work alongside social workers, care managers, GPs, nurses, pharmacists, occupational therapists, physiotherapists, local authority teams and other health and social care professionals.

Good communication between everyone involved in someone's care helps reduce risk, identify changes early, and provide more consistent support. See how we support professional referrals →

Safeguarding

Free from abuse, neglect and avoidable harm

Everyone has the right to live free from abuse, neglect, exploitation and avoidable harm. Safeguarding is part of every Dòchas employee's responsibility. Our staff are trained to recognise concerns, report them and follow the appropriate safeguarding procedures, they are not expected to investigate concerns themselves. They are expected to notice, record and report.

Where necessary, Dòchas works with local authorities, healthcare professionals, Police Scotland, the Care Inspectorate and other appropriate agencies. Safeguarding does not mean taking control away from people unnecessarily, it means protecting rights, responding appropriately to concerns, and involving the individual as far as possible.

Listening to clients and families

Feedback shapes how we improve

We want people to tell us how they experience our service, including positive feedback, suggestions, concerns and complaints. We don't see complaints as something that should be hidden or discouraged, they can show us where something has gone wrong, or where we need to communicate better, change a process or improve our service.

People using Dòchas should feel able to raise concerns without worrying that doing so will affect the care they receive. Read about how to speak up →

Supporting our staff

Good care depends on the people providing it

That's why Dòchas also places importance on how staff are recruited, trained, supervised and supported. We expect high standards from our employees, but we also recognise our responsibility to give them the tools and support needed to meet those standards. Our approach includes:

Safer recruitment

Appropriate PVG checks and SSSC requirements where applicable.

Induction & training

Ongoing learning, clear policies and professional expectations.

Supervision & support

Support when concerns arise, opportunities to ask questions, and a culture where speaking up is encouraged.

Staff wellbeing matters because supported staff are better able to provide safe and compassionate care. See what it's like to work at Dòchas →

Equality, diversity and inclusion

Every person treated fairly and with respect

This applies to clients, employees, families, applicants and professionals working with Dòchas. We do not believe that good care can exist where discrimination, harassment or prejudice is tolerated. We respect people's backgrounds, identities, beliefs, cultures, relationships and individual circumstances.

Equality does not always mean treating everyone identically, sometimes people need different forms of support to achieve fair and meaningful outcomes.

What you can expect from Dòchas

When you choose Dòchas, you should be able to expect us to:

  • Listen to you
  • Treat you with dignity
  • Respect your home
  • Protect your privacy
  • Involve you in decisions
  • Encourage your independence
  • Explain what we are doing
  • Provide care safely
  • Take concerns seriously
  • Communicate honestly
  • Respect your choices
  • Recognise when your needs change

Work with other professionals where appropriate. And remember that your care is about you.

Looking ahead

Dòchas is still growing

As we grow, we want to remain ambitious about the quality of care we provide, without losing the values that led to the organisation being created in the first place. Growth should never mean becoming distant from the people we support. It should mean creating the systems, teams and leadership needed to provide excellent care to more people, while protecting the culture behind the service.

We want Dòchas to be recognised not simply for providing home care, but for the way that care feels: safe, respectful, professional, compassionate, personal, and hopeful.

The Dòchas difference

Every person has a story

There are many organisations that provide care. What we want people to remember about Dòchas is how we approach it. We don't believe people should disappear behind care plans, rotas or diagnoses. Every home has its own rhythm. Every family is different. Every person's idea of independence is different. And every visit is an opportunity to make someone's day safer, easier or simply a little better.

That's why one principle runs through everything we do:

"Every visit belongs to the client."That is the Dòchas Way
